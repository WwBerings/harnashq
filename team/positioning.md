# HARNAS positioning: three directions

23 October 2026. Replaces "AI agents for the busywork", which sells the tool and not a reason to buy.

## The starting point

The buyer is a small firm (2 to 50 people) that already suspects it should use AI. Among Dutch micro firms that considered AI, CBS lists what stops them:

- lack of experience: 71.6%
- privacy: 48.8%
- legal worries: 42.7%
- cost: 20.3%

So the buyer is not short of money or interest. They don't know how to start, and they're afraid of what happens to their files and their liability. Their real alternatives are doing nothing, trying ChatGPT on their own, or a generic AI agency that ships something untested. Every headline below names one of those barriers, not a feature.

Fit check: every headline was measured in Helvetica Neue Bold with the site's -0.05em tracking and fits two lines inside the hero's 9.5em measure (the binding limit at 8vw). The Dutch line in direction 1 is the tightest at 9.06em, and it relies on the `text-wrap: balance` already on `h1`.

## Direction 1: Safety first

| | EN | NL |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | AI without giving your files away. | AI zonder je gegevens af te staan. |
| Supporting line | Built inside Microsoft 365, Google Workspace or your own software. Nothing is stored anywhere else. | Gebouwd in Microsoft 365, Google Workspace of je eigen software. Niets wordt ergens anders opgeslagen. |

- **Barrier it answers:** privacy (48.8%) and legal worries (42.7%).
- **The story:** you can use AI on confidential work, because we build it inside the systems you already trust and a person signs off on everything that goes out.
- **Main risk:** privacy is a reason not to say no, not a reason to say yes. It gets the firm past the first objection but gives no motive to start. It also invites the hardest scrutiny: the model provider still processes the data as a subprocessor, so every word has to survive a privacy lawyer reading it.

## Direction 2: Know-how first

| | EN | NL |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | AI, without learning AI first. | AI, zonder eerst AI te leren. |
| Supporting line | We find the task worth automating, build it in your systems and keep it running. | Wij vinden de taak die het waard is, bouwen hem in je systemen en houden hem draaiend. |

- **Barrier it answers:** lack of experience (71.6%), the biggest one by far.
- **The story:** you know AI could save your firm time, but you don't have the hours to work out how, so we do the finding, building and maintaining, and you only decide.
- **Main risk:** "we do it for you" is what every agency says once you get past their headline, so the difference has to come from the page below it. It can also draw buyers who want to hand off the thinking but not pay for it. The free scan and the fixed price filter for that.

## Direction 3: Proof first

| | EN | NL |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Proven on your own work first. | Eerst bewezen op je eigen werk. |
| Supporting line | Tested on three months of your past work. Under 95% right, the last invoice is on us. | We testen op drie maanden van je eigen werk. Onder 95% juist? Dan vervalt de laatste termijn. |

- **Barrier it answers:** legal worries (42.7%) and the fear of mistakes underneath the lack of experience. A firm without AI experience can't judge whether an agent works. This hands that judgement to a test on their own work.
- **The story:** before anything goes live, you see how often it gets your own work right, and if it misses the mark, you don't pay the last invoice.
- **Main risk:** the guarantee carries the whole page, so it has to hold in every contract, including tasks where "right" is hard to measure. With no cases yet, the 95% is a promise, not a result. It reads as confident only as long as the founder can deliver it every time. It also suggests a bigger, slower project than "one task, one price" does.

---

## Recommendation

Lead with direction 2, "AI, without learning AI first", because it names the barrier 71.6% of these buyers give and beats their most likely alternative: doing nothing, or trying it themselves. Make direction 1 the first section under the hero and direction 3 the proof in "How it works". Privacy and legal worry are the next two barriers, and the test on their own work is the one thing no competing agency offers, so both belong on the page, just not in the H1. Confidence: moderate. If the site ends up talking almost only to law firms, swap in direction 1 as the headline, because there confidentiality is the objection that ends the conversation.

## Words and claims to avoid

**Hype vocabulary:** revolutionize, unlock, supercharge, game-changer, 10x, AI-powered, transform, seamless, effortless, cutting-edge, next-level, smart/intelligent as a selling word, on autopilot, "the future of work".

**What the 14 competitor sites all say (LANDSCAPE.md), and so blends in:**
- "Get a demo", "Book a demo", "No credit card required", "Log in"
- "Trusted by", logo rows, "used by teams at"
- Proof by number with nothing counted behind it ("save 10 hours a week", "3x faster")
- A capsule badge over the headline ("New", "Now with agents")
- Benefit-only headlines that could sit on any of the 14 sites

**Claims we cannot make (yet), or that are not true:**
- "Nothing leaves your systems" or "Your data stays home" as an absolute. The model provider processes the data as a subprocessor. Say "nothing is stored anywhere else" and "never used for training".
- "GDPR-compliant", "100% secure" or "certified". Say what we do (processor agreement under art. 28, a full list of subprocessors) and let the firm's lawyer judge.
- Any customer, case, quote or measured saving until the first pilot has its before and after numbers.
- Any statistic without a source on cbs.nl or similar. The old "61% tried, 25% disappointed" figures are out.
- "Replaces staff" or "fully autonomous". A person signs off. That's the promise.
- The founder's background with large software organisations, and any person or client name.

**Words that sell the tool instead of the reason:** "AI agents" in the headline, "busywork", and in Dutch "handwerk", which reads as craft, not drudgery.
